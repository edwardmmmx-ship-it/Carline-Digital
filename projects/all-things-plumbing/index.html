The ATP approach

We know
renovations.

Most plumbers just do the job. At ATP, we help you understand it. Over 10 years, we've seen every renovation mistake — and we'll make sure you don't make them.

This is real expertise from a plumber who has been inside hundreds of Auckland homes. We share it openly — because informed customers make better decisions, and better decisions lead to better outcomes for everyone.

Talk to us first
01
Invest in your shower mixer
The shower mixer sits behind the wall. If it fails, replacing it is a major job. Buy a quality mixer — it's the one fixture you should never go cheap on. This is especially important because the mixer regulates your hot/cold balance and water pressure.
02
Quality kitchen taps pay for themselves
The kitchen mixer is the most used tap in your home. Cheap mixers wear fast and cost more in the long run. The same goes for in-wall cisterns — they cost more upfront and take 2–3 extra hours to install, so know that before you commit.
03
Stick to known shower brands
Budget acrylic kit-set showers vary wildly in quality. Avoid the Asian imports — for a little more, well-known brands deliver significantly better quality. The difference in longevity more than justifies the price.
04
Refacing beats replacing in kitchens
Replacing kitchen cabinet doors, drawer faces and panels can transform the look of a kitchen for a fraction of a full replacement. Add a new benchtop and you have a "brand new" kitchen in a matter of hours, saving thousands.
05
Get fixtures on-site before tradespeople start
Plumbers need to know where fixtures will sit to rough in behind walls correctly. If specifications aren't confirmed early, expensive corrections follow. Have everything chosen and on-site before the first tradesperson arrives — this is the single best way to avoid blowouts.
06
Minimise changes once you've started
Every change during a renovation costs more than it would have before it started. Once pipes are in walls and concrete is poured, changes mean undoing work. Spend the time upfront making good decisions — it's always cheaper.
